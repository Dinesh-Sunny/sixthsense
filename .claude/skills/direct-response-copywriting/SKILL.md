# Direct-Response Copywriting

Distilled from a 1997 Halbert/Carlton session, Harry Dry's method, and a modern ad-scripting masterclass. "Letter" = any long-form sales message (page, email, VSL). Copy is the lifeblood of the business.

## Workflow

0. Read `VOICE.md` (repo root) first. Its settings (voice, vocabulary, banned words, claims policy, CTA form) are binding and override generic advice below.
1. Name the reader's current attitude (A), desired attitude (B), and the single action. Lead-gen pieces sell the response, not the product.
2. Research before writing: evidence the desire outside your marketing; mine accommodation, failed solution, nearly-didn't-buy, their word, the moment. Place on awareness × sophistication.
3. Draft in Carlton order: bullets → opening → close → headline last.
4. Test every key line: visualize? falsify? nobody else can say it? Then run `references/critique-checklist.md` (includes ethics guardrails) before showing anything.
5. Recommend tests, not certainties. Copy is a process, not an event.
6. Always finish with the `humanizer` skill (copywriting mode) on the final copy before delivering it.

## Response hierarchy (fix in this order)

1. **List/audience**: biggest lever. Hottest prospects already bought something similar.
2. **Offer**: what they get, on what terms, with what risk removed.
3. **Copy**: headline, story, bullets, close.
4. **Presentation**: format, grabbers, delivery.

Results make no sense? Distrust the data first (was it sent, to whom?).

## Level 1: Fundamentals

- Talk about them, not you. Credentials come after the benefit.
- Feature → benefit → deeper benefit. Never make the reader translate.
- Make benefits concrete: "10 minutes instead of an hour, then a movie with the kids."
- Say "get/discover," not "learn." Sell results, not homework.
- Specific beats round: "up 247%" beats "doubled." Don't talk, point: facts and evidence over adjectives.
- Concrete beats abstract: if you can't picture it, they won't remember it.
- Never write a line a competitor could sign.
- Two-second test: the reader gets it before "two Mississippi."
- Repeat key points ~3×, phrased differently.
- Plain, sincere prose. Every word not working for you works against you; ideas too (avoid "and").
- Short paragraphs, eye relief, ragged text, subheads.
- Write with genuine passion for the product; edit down later.
- Do the full selling job every time, even to eager prospects.

## Level 2: Craft

- Headline/hook decides who reads; the body sells. Write it last, and with line 2.
- Beats: hook → problem → promise → mechanism → objection → proof → offer → risk reversal → CTA.
- Agitate the consequence they've accommodated, not the symptom. Promise before mechanism.
- Bullets are the engine. Write all of them; one obscure bullet often closes.
- Story sells: relatable person, pain, discovery, specific results.
- Greased slide: each line exists to get the next read. Never end a page on a full stop.
- Subheads tell the whole story for skimmers.
- Lead-gen copy stays blind: sell curiosity and end result, not mechanism.
- Editorial (article-style) format beats obvious ads. Call info offers a "book."

## Level 3: Offer and mechanics

- Treat the proposition as a test variable: price, terms, deferred billing, guarantee.
- Price by payback: "it pays for itself in two weeks."
- Remove fear: longer guarantees cut refunds; deferred charging lifts sales.
- Give a true, believable reason for every deal.
- Personalize; make it look personal, not mass-produced.

## Level 4: Systems

- Two-step: cheap hand-raiser → high-impact package → phone call (~5× sales).
- Record your best sales calls; turn the "concert pitch" into copy and training.
- Run a family of ads; knock off your own winner before competitors do.
- Back-end: instant upsell, step-down offers, recorded-seminar bonuses, continuity.
- Kill the tail: replace post-sale personal service with recorded/written assets.
