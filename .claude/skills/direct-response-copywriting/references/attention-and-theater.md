# Attention, Grabbers & Theater

Marketing is a matter of impact. People see thousands of messages and remember the *look* of a promotion, not what it said (Ed Mayer). Don't economize on hand-raisers — spend to stand out to people who already responded.

## Getting opened / noticed

- Look personal: real stamp, plain return address (no company name), typed/personalized name, signature in blue. Mass-mail markers (bulk indicia, labels) get sorted into the trash pile.
- Personalization: name in the headline and salutation ("Terry, please read the headline at the top of this page"). Personalized "imagine the newspaper said…" subheads make readers picture the result.
- Avoid teaser copy that reveals the contents; curiosity is what gets the envelope (or email) opened.
- Lumpy/odd formats: tubes with an object inside, oversized packages, priority/express envelopes, "photo enclosed" on the outside (a real photo inside — big tested lift).
- Handwritten-style personalized note on the outside, e.g. "don't read this until you're alone."
- Gimmicky "cute" stuff (fake stains, pre-crumpled letters) — test, but cute rarely beats relevant.

## Grabbers + segues

Attach an object to the top of the letter, then segue it into the pitch:

> "As you can see, I've attached [object]. Why? Two reasons. First, I have something important to tell you and wanted to be sure this caught your attention. Second, since this is about [benefit], [object] seemed especially fitting…"

- Money is the best grabber: real dollar bill, foreign notes (Japanese yen for a product discovered in Japan; a bundle of Pacific Rim currencies to dramatize untapped export sales), two pennies as "your change."
- Practice: any object can be segued (lottery ticket → leaving income to chance; rubber band → stretching the same resources; band-aid → quick fixes vs. real solutions; balloon → hot air from ordinary consultants). Generate several, pick the most relevant.
- **Dollar-bill letter close**: "Spend it, give it away — but I hope you frame it, because two years from now when someone asks, you can say that was the first dollar you got from your association with [me]… and since then you've made far more."

## Theater

Go beyond the paper:
- Delivery: express/priority package, courier, telegram-style, hand delivery for high-value prospects.
- Hand-raiser package: priority envelope → one-page personalized cover letter with grabber → sealed inner envelope containing the main pitch. Cover letter's job is only to get the sales letter read now.
- Tear sheet: page that looks torn from a newspaper (editorial-style article about you), with a personalized sticky note: "Joe — thought you'd want to see this. —J." Newsprint beats magazine stock; personalization is the whole point. Running the ad for real in a small paper adds legitimacy.
- High-value B2B: photo of the prospect's own storefront ("something in this picture repels 70% of your customers"), a mock-up of their own ad/book cover framed and gift-wrapped.
- Celebrity: faded/"Love Boat"-tier celebrities are affordable and lift response. Relevant sexy imagery lifts response in many categories — but relevance matters; irrelevant gimmicks (a random bodybuilder) don't.
- Media formats: print usually beats audio, audio beats video for the *selling* step (reader controls pace, no equipment). Recorded talks work best as a follow-up asset or to replace in-person seminars.

## Envelope-to-close sequence for sales letters

1. Envelope/subject gets opened (curiosity, personal look).
2. Grabber + headline get the first line read.
3. Greased slide carries them through.
4. Close makes one easy action.
