# Funnels, Follow-up & Back-end

## Lead generation (two-step)

- Ad/mailing's only job: get the hand-raise (free call, form, reply). No order coupon needed.
- Offer a free "book"/report or recording; capture name and address via an easy free channel.
- Recorded-message capture lines get 50–90% capture; free to call beats paid-call (though paid-call qualifies harder).
- Test the lead-gen piece as direct mail to a sample of a publication's list: if mail gets ~90% of break-even, the ad in the publication may return 1.5–2×.
- Funnel variant (Don Alm): tiny ad → paid call/record → letter selling a video for a refundable $20 deposit → video pitches → follow-ups. Prospects pay to see the pitch; ~70% never reclaim the deposit.
- Multi-page ads that reprint a winning long letter in a targeted magazine often beat shorter ads; mail and print share the same copy.

## Follow-up sequences

- Follow up until they buy, die, or it stops paying. Half of sales can come after the 2nd step.
- Step 1 should be highest-impact (priority package + dollar-bill cover letter). Re-send the best letter rather than a weaker one.
- Change the look each step (envelope size, color, format), not just the cover sheet; same-looking pieces get discarded on sight.
- Vary the voice: letter from assistant/secretary ("he doesn't know I'm sending this"), from a customer, with a real photo. Different messenger can convert people who ignored you — keep it truthful.
- Endorsement/exposé ads reinforce prospects sitting on earlier letters.

## The phone

- After the priority package, call: "I sent you a package with a dollar bill attached — did it arrive? (yes) Did you get a chance to read it? (yes) What did you like best? … We've had lots of calls; I hadn't heard from you — any questions?"
- Rule of thumb: letter + call ≈ 5× sales of letter alone.
- Do the first ~100 calls yourself, record them, capture the best ("concert pitch"), and turn them into training recordings covering the ~10 most common objections.
- Test 100 at a time: send 100, call, learn why people didn't buy, revise, next 100. You're buying information as well as sales.
- Emergency bailout: dollar-bill letter to your existing list + follow-up call.

## Seminars & recordings

- In-person/live pitch converting 25–30%? Capture it on video/audio and deliver it to the lists of people who attend similar events — skip the travel.
- Expensive-seminar model: promote a very high-priced seminar to your list; record it; immediately offer the recordings free with a long (multi-year/lifetime) subscription to those who wanted to attend but couldn't afford it.
- Record recurring calls/teleconferences; transcribe into newsletter content and products.

## Upsell & cross-sell

- Most receptive moment = right after purchase. Upsell before the product even arrives: reassurance "stick letter" + "since you ordered X, a special deal on Y if you act now."
- Every sale should be immediately followed by an additional offer (phone and mail).
- Renew subscriptions early (right after signup) and with the same premium-intensive effort as the original sale.

## Step-down & reactivation

- Non-buyers raised their hand; price is usually the reason. Mail them: cheaper stripped package, partial product, half-price, payments.
- Old/neglected leads: test in small batches (100–1,000/week), one or two shots, then let go.
- Can come from a different brand/persona/location — you owe no explanation for why they got the letter.

## Continuity

- Goal: recurring revenue with minimal personal labor. Bundle newsletter + monthly recording + bonus reports + group calls into one charge.
- Most valuable continuity content = what they really want: a fresh done-for-you promotion each month (or two), not more lessons.
- Make it addictive and personal: people subscribe for what you can do for them, but renew because they like you — share personal stories, photos, a cause you champion.
- Tease next month's content in each issue; consider a monthly "sales letter" for what they've already bought so value stays visible.
- Expect churn (people leave the industry); some "let sleeping dogs lie" — don't over-remind about the charge.

## Killing the tail

- Tail = any servicing after the check clears (calls, consults, critiques). It consumes the founder and rarely increases front-end sales.
- Replace personal service with equal-value recorded/written assets: indexed reports answering the top questions, video critiques, group calls, fax/email-only questions (written questions eliminate most trivial ones).
- Test offers with and without tail; often same price, same sales.
