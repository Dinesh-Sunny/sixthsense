# Offers, Pricing, Guarantees, Urgency

## The proposition is a test variable

Variations of "you give me X, I give you Y":
- Plain sale → add 30-day money-back → longer guarantee → double-your-money-back (conditional)
- Pay over N months; half now / half in 45 days; installments with built-in interest
- Post-dated check / deferred processing ("we won't cash your check or charge your card for 30–60 days")
- Bill-me-later (most profitable for information sellers at scale, but requires collection capability)
- Cash discount option alongside payments (~40% may take it)
- Free + small shipping fee for a lead magnet (e.g. "free, just $3 shipping") — test vs. higher fees
- Mail the same winner until it stops paying; test challengers in parallel.

## Pricing

- **Payback framing**: how soon does the buyer earn the price back? If in weeks, price becomes irrelevant — "a ticket for the ride." Lets you test far higher prices than the model you copied.
- Test price points (e.g. 577/677/777) and test radically higher packages.
- Tiers: basic / gold / platinum — differ by bonuses and subscription length, not tail.
- $97/month is a well-tested continuity price point.
- Factoring: sell installment receivables to a finance company at a 3–10% discount to get cash now and offload collection risk.

## Guarantees & refunds

- Longer guarantees reduce refunds (30-day mattress guarantee → 90-day nearly eliminated returns).
- Most refund-seekers decide before ordering; the rest quit when they discover real work is required.
- **Conditional double-your-money-back** for implementation products: "Give it an honest try. If it doesn't work, send proof you did it (copy of your ad, business license, letterhead) and get double back." Few claim; those who try usually succeed. Keep the proof reasonable, not onerous.
- **The sale isn't done until the refund window closes.** Sell at every step: thank-you, post-purchase reassurance ("stick letter"), check-in calls, a free bonus promised after implementation.
- **Bonding** reduces refunds: personal contact, letters, offers of help — it's harder to stiff a friend.
- Dollar-bill "frame this" note in the delivery package (see attention-and-theater.md) doubles as reassurance.

## Urgency & reason-why

- Every promotion needs a reason for the deal, and the reason must be believable — and true. Examples of real reasons: anniversary/birthday sale, limited-hours private sale for past customers, factory deal valid one week, timely event (season, big game, news).
- Exclusivity: private invitation, customers only, limited spots, "special deal for [city] people only."
- Deadline language: "If you set this aside, odds you'll find it again tomorrow are slim."
- Watch hard caps ("only 200 can join") — they limit sequential mailings; prefer time windows or honest capacity limits.
- Half-price is the most powerful discount for re-activating fence-sitters; protect full-price buyers (give it a separate legitimate reason, e.g. a stripped-down edition).

## Packaging the offer

- "Book" outperforms report/newsletter for front-end info offers. Newsletters are the hardest info product to sell — people want the answer now, not in installments.
- Piling on value works, but bonuses built from personal service create tail — prefer recorded/written equivalents.
- Strip-down step-down: remove half the package, sell at half price; repeat down to a single item while mailings still pay.
