# Research & Buyer Psychology

## Sales detective work (before writing)

- **Interview the client/expert repeatedly.** Owners are too close to know their real hot buttons. The winning angle is often a tidbit they mention only on the third conversation (Carlton's "one-legged golfer" — the origin story of a swing technique became the headline that ran for years).
- **Let facts "gel."** Gathering and staring at the material often takes longer than writing. Wait for the angle where everything clicks; when there is none, it's ditch-digging — just write it.
- **Call non-buyers** within ~2 days of their not buying: ask why not, what they didn't like. Rewrite from field feedback, not guesses.
- **Call your best prospects and pitch them yourself, recorded.** Repeat until you forget you're recording and nail it — that transcript is the heart of the letter (crash course in copy).
- **Analyze who actually buys** (regression on existing buyers: car model, zip code profile, income, prior purchases). Buyers are often the opposite of who the company assumes.
- **Find the triggering event.** People buy when something happens (in-laws visiting → carpet cleaning), not when the need first appears. Write to the event; give a time-sensitive reason to act now.
- **Know the idiom/audience.** Specialist audience (bass fishing list, enthusiast magazine) → jargon-rich "muscle writing," jump straight in, buddy-to-buddy. Broad audience → ease in, establish interest before pitching. Don't run the same ad in a general and a specialist outlet.

## What people really want

- **Universal wants** (Schwab's "Colonel's lady and Judy O'Grady"): everyone — rich or poor — wants love, respect, comfort, security, to feel special. Don't scoff at these needs.
- **Two reasons for every purchase**: the logical one they give, and the real (emotional) one. Sell the real one; supply the logical one so they can justify it.
- **Make them feel special**: singled out, insider deal, better treatment than everyone else, first to know. Status and self-esteem go up.
- **Old wounds**: people carry slights (rejection, bullies, lack of respect). "Walk in and the room goes quiet; confidence shines from your eyes like a searchlight" — give them the swagger.
- **Simplicity & less stress** are near-universal desires.
- **They want it done, not to learn how.** Nobody wants to learn engine repair; they want the engine fixed. Sell the outcome; offer "done-for-you" as the premium path.
- **Must-haves vs. nice-to-haves**: map both, prioritize bullets where your strengths meet must-haves.

## Inertia & the comfort zone

- Treat the prospect as a "slug" — getting them to act takes enormous effort. Every step must be easy and compelling; once in motion, hit them again while they're "in heat."
- People fight to stay in their comfort zone (Halbert's amended Maslow). Expect most buyers not to implement; design products and follow-ups to be user-friendly and "in their face" so more succeed (more testimonials, fewer refunds).
- Delay kills the sale: anything that makes them postpone the decision (e.g., "listen to this tape first") can halve response — test before adding.
- Curiosity is the strongest response driver. Don't give away what's inside (avoid teaser copy that reveals the offer on the envelope/subject line).
